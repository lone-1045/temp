# The Mountain of Stories (new version): narration script and AI image prompts

Theme: Be Wise, Be Bold, Rise to Glory and Grace. Poster rules: .pptx, 4 slides, 1 AI image per slide, 5-10 minutes, team of 3-5. Deadline: 5 October 2026.

## How this answers the feedback

- **One story, not three:** a single storyline from start to finish. Mei's grandfather goes missing in a storm, and she must find him.
- **Fewer people:** only 3 characters (Mei, Grandpa, Narrator).
- **A real story:** a problem, a risky journey, a rescue and an ending. Nobody stands up to lecture. Wisdom and boldness are shown by what Mei does: she stops to listen (wise), then crawls the ridge and crosses the flooded stream (bold).
- **Length:** about 1149 words, roughly 8 minutes at a natural storytelling pace (7.7 min at 150 words per minute, 8.5 at 135, 9.6 at 120). If you run past 10 minutes, speak a little faster or trim a line or two.

## Voice cast

- NARRATOR: all story text (split between two students if you have 4-5 people, e.g. one per two slides)
- MEI
- GRANDPA

## Slide 1: Grandpa's Stories

**NARRATOR:** High above a small village, where the clouds sleep on the rooftops, stands a mountain called Cloud Peak. In that village lived a twelve-year-old girl named Mei, and her grandfather.

**NARRATOR:** For forty years, Grandpa had been the mountain guide. He knew every rock and every stream of Cloud Peak. But he never gave directions. Not once. Instead, every night after dinner, he told stories.

**GRANDPA:** Once upon a time, there was a crooked pine on the high ridge. When the great winds came, it did not fight them. It bowed. And because it bowed, it never broke.

**NARRATOR:** Mei loved her grandfather. But she did not love his stories.

**MEI:** Grandpa, I don't need another story about a tree. I need a map. Tell me where the path goes, and I'll find it myself.

**GRANDPA:** A map tells you where the path is, Mei. A story teaches you how to walk it. One day, you'll see the difference.

**NARRATOR:** Mei only sighed. And the stories kept coming, year after year. The Crooked Pine that bowed to the wind. The Three Brothers Rock, three stones leaning on one another because none could stand alone. The Singing Stream, which always runs downhill to the village. And the Hermit's Cave, where the mountain keeps a dry place for those who know how to wait.

**NARRATOR:** Mei knew them all by heart. But she had never once truly listened.

**NARRATOR:** Until one autumn morning, when Grandpa climbed Cloud Peak alone to check the trail markers. By evening, he had not come home. By night, a storm broke over the mountain. The rescue team said it was too dangerous to search in the dark. They would go at first light. But Mei knew that first light might be too late.

*About 288 words.*

## Slide 2: Be Wise

**NARRATOR:** Mei's first instinct was to run. Her boots were on. Her hand was on the door. But then she stopped. And for the first time in her life, she did the hardest thing of all: she did nothing. She sat down, closed her eyes, and listened. Not to the rain. To the stories.

**MEI:** The Crooked Pine bows to the wind, so it never breaks. The Three Brothers lean on one another. The Singing Stream runs downhill. And the Hermit's Cave keeps a dry place for those who wait.

**NARRATOR:** Then her eyes flew open. They were not just stories. They were a map. For twelve years, Grandpa had been drawing her a map that could only be read by someone who listened.

**NARRATOR:** A guide like Grandpa would never fight a storm. He would bow, like the pine, and shelter in the Hermit's Cave until morning. And the stories showed the way: the pine on the high ridge, the Three Brothers Rock, across the Singing Stream, and up to the cave.

**MEI:** He's not lost. He's waiting. And I know where.

**NARRATOR:** Mei could have rushed into the dark. Instead, she did what a wise climber does. She packed a raincoat, a torch, a rope, hot water, and Grandpa's warmest blanket. Then she wrote down the whole route, every word of every story, and pinned it to the village notice board, so the rescue team could follow.

**NARRATOR:** She had always thought listening was the same as doing nothing. Tonight, it was the most important thing she had ever done. Then she opened the door, and stepped into the storm.

*About 265 words.*

## Slide 3: Be Bold

**NARRATOR:** The wind howled like a living thing. The rain stung her cheeks. Her torch lit only a few steps ahead, and beyond that, the mountain was a wall of black.

**NARRATOR:** At the fork, the wide, easy path went left, straight towards the cliff. But the story said: find the pine. So Mei took the narrow, slippery trail on the right, until at last she reached the high ridge, and there it stood: the Crooked Pine, bent almost double by the wind, but alive.

**NARRATOR:** Beyond the pine, the ridge was bare, and the wind could knock a grown man off his feet. Mei was terrified. Her legs would not move. Then she remembered the pine. It did not fight. It bowed. So Mei got down on her hands and knees, and crawled along the ridge, inch by inch, with the storm screaming over her head.

**NARRATOR:** She passed the Three Brothers Rock, three stones leaning together in the dark, and felt a little less alone. Then she reached the Singing Stream. But it was not singing anymore. It was roaring, swollen with rain. The only way across was a line of slippery stones.

**NARRATOR:** Mei tied her rope around a tree. She took one deep breath. And she stepped into the water. The current dragged at her legs. But she did not stop. Step by step, stone by stone, she crossed.

**NARRATOR:** Above her, a faint orange glow flickered inside the rock. The Hermit's Cave. Slipping, falling, getting up again, Mei climbed the last stretch and cried out into the darkness.

**MEI:** Grandpa!

**NARRATOR:** For a moment, there was only the wind. And then, from inside the cave, a weak voice.

**GRANDPA:** Mei? Is that really you? You came all this way?

**MEI:** I listened, Grandpa. I listened to every story.

**NARRATOR:** Grandpa had twisted his ankle, and he had done exactly what his own story said: he had bowed, found shelter, and waited. Mei wrapped the blanket around his shoulders, and they sat together in the dry place the mountain had kept for them, while the storm raged, and slowly died.

*About 343 words.*

## Slide 4: Rise to Glory and Grace

**NARRATOR:** At dawn, the storm broke. Mei helped Grandpa to the mouth of the cave, and together they stepped out into a world made of gold. Below them, a sea of clouds glowed pink and orange, as the sun rose over Cloud Peak.

**GRANDPA:** Forty years I guided people to the top of this mountain. And do you know what I learned? Glory is reaching the top. But grace is how you come back down, together.

**NARRATOR:** Mei put her shoulder under his arm, and they began the long walk home, following the Singing Stream downhill, just as the story promised. Before long, they heard voices: the rescue team, climbing the trail with Mei's note in their hands.

**NARRATOR:** That night, the whole village gathered for the lantern festival. And Mei did something she had never done before. She stood up in front of everyone, and she began.

**MEI:** Once upon a time, there was a crooked pine on the high ridge. When the great winds came, it did not fight them. It bowed. And because it bowed, it never broke.

**NARRATOR:** The children sat down on the ground to listen. And in the front row, Grandpa smiled. Because he knew the mountain had never been made of rock and stone. It was made of stories, each one a rope thrown down by someone who had climbed before, for someone wise enough to listen, and bold enough to climb.

**NARRATOR:** Be wise enough to listen. Be bold enough to climb. And rise together, with glory and with grace. The End.

*About 253 words.*
